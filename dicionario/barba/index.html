Barba

Easton · em inglês: Beard

O modo de usar a barba era expressamente prescrito aos judeus (Levítico 19:27; 21:5). Daí o sentido da descrição de Ezequiel (5:1-4) da “navalha”, isto é, dos agentes de uma providência irada usados contra a nação culpada dos judeus. Fazia parte da higiene diária de um judeu ungir a barba com óleo e perfume (Salmos 133:2). As barbas eram aparadas com o cuidado mais meticuloso (2 Samuel 19:24), e seu descuido era sinal de profunda tristeza (Isaías 15:2; Jeremias 41:5). O costume era raspar ou arrancar os pelos como sinal de luto (Isaías 50:6; Jeremias 48:37; Esdras 9:3). As barbas dos embaixadores de Davi foram cortadas por Hanum (2 Samuel 10:4) como marca de desonra.

Texto original (inglês)

The mode of wearing it was definitely prescribed to the Jews (Lev. 19:27; 21:5). Hence the import of Ezekiel's (5:1-4) description of the "razor" i.e., the agents of an angry providence being used against the guilty nation of the Jews. It was a part of a Jew's daily toilet to anoint his beard with oil and perfume (Ps. 133:2). Beards were trimmed with the most fastidious care (2 Sam. 19:24), and their neglet was an indication of deep sorrow (Isa. 15:2; Jer. 41:5). The custom was to shave or pluck off the hair as a sign of mourning (Isa. 50:6; Jer. 48:37; Ezra 9:3). The beards of David's ambassadors were cut off by hanun (2 Sam. 10:4) as a mark of indignity.
