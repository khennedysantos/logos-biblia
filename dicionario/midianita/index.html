Midianita

Easton · em inglês: Midianite

Uma tribo árabe descendente de Midiã. Habitava principalmente o deserto ao norte da península da Arábia. A península do Sinai servia de pastagem para os seus rebanhos. Eram praticamente os governantes da Arábia, sendo a tribo dominante. Como todos os árabes, eram um povo nômade. Cedo se dedicaram a atividades comerciais. Foi a uma das suas caravanas que José foi vendido (Gênesis 37:28, 36). A próxima menção a eles está relacionada à fuga de Moisés do Egito (Êxodo 2:15-21). Aqui, em Midiã, Moisés tornou-se servo e, depois, genro de Reuel, ou Jetro, o sacerdote. Após o Êxodo, os midianitas foram amigáveis com os israelitas enquanto estes percorreram apenas as suas pastagens periféricas a oeste da Arabá; mas, quando, tendo passado a extremidade sul de Edom, entraram na própria terra de Midiã, aliaram-se a Balaque, rei de Moabe, numa conspiração contra eles (Números 22:4-7). Balaão, que havia sido chamado para amaldiçoar Israel e tendo fracassado totalmente nisso, foi dispensado pelo rei de Moabe; contudo, permaneceu entre os midianitas e os induziu a entrar em correspondência com os israelitas, de modo a associá-los às orgias licenciosas ligadas ao culto de Baal-Peor. Este conselho astuto prevaleceu. Os israelitas tomaram parte na festa pagã e, assim, atraíram sobre si uma maldição de fato. A sua apostasia trouxe sobre eles um severo castigo. Irrompeu entre eles uma praga, e mais de vinte e quatro mil do povo perecerem (Números 25:9). Mas os midianitas não deveriam ficar impunes. Foi anunciada contra eles uma terrível vingança. Mil guerreiros de cada tribo, sob a liderança de Finéias, saíram contra eles. Os midianitas foram completamente derrotados. Suas cidades foram consumidas pelo fogo, cinco dos seus reis foram mortos e toda a nação foi destruída (Josué 13:21, 22). Balaão também morreu à espada, recebendo o “salário da sua injustiça” (Números 31:8; 2 Pedro 2:15). Toda a região a leste do Jordão, agora conquistada pelos israelitas (veja SIHOM; OGUE), foi dividida entre as duas tribos de Rúben e Gade e a meia tribo de Manassés.

Texto original (inglês)

An Arabian tribe descended from Midian. They inhabited principally the desert north of the peninsula of Arabia. The peninsula of Sinai was the pasture-ground for their flocks. They were virtually the rulers of Arabia, being the dominant tribe. Like all Arabians, they were a nomad people. They early engaged in commercial pursuits. It was to one of their caravans that Joseph was sold (Gen. 37:28, 36). The next notice of them is in connection with Moses' flight from Egypt (Ex. 2:15-21). Here in Midian Moses became the servant and afterwards the son-in-law of Reuel or Jethro, the priest. After the Exodus, the Midianites were friendly to the Israelites so long as they traversed only their outlying pasture-ground on the west of the Arabah; but when, having passed the southern end of Edom, they entered into the land of Midian proper, they joined with Balak, the king of Moab, in a conspiracy against them (Num. 22:4-7). Balaam, who had been sent for to curse Israel, having utterly failed to do so, was dismissed by the king of Moab; nevertheless he still tarried among the Midianites, and induced them to enter into correspondence with the Israelites, so as to bring them into association with them in the licentious orgies connected with the worship of Baal-Peor. This crafty counsel prevailed. The Israelites took part in the heathen festival, and so brought upon themselves a curse indeed. Their apostasy brought upon them a severe punishment. A plague broke out amongst them, and more than twenty-four thousand of the people perished (Num. 25:9). But the Midianites were not to be left unpunished. A terrible vengeance was denounced against them. A thousand warriors from each tribe, under the leadership of Phinehas, went forth against them. The Midianites were utterly routed. Their cities were consumed by fire, five of their kings were put to death, and the whole nation was destroyed (Josh. 13:21, 22). Balaam also perished by the sword, receiving the "wages of his unrighteousness" (Num. 31:8; 2 Pet. 2:15). The whole of the country on the east of Jordan, now conquered by the Israelites (see [400]SIHON; [401]OG), was divided between the two tribes of Reuben and Gad and the half tribe of Manasseh.
