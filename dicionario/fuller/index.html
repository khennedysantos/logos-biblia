Fuller

Easton

A palavra “full” (pisoar) vem do anglo-saxão fullian, que significa “branquear”. Pisoar é prensar ou lavar tecido num moinho. Esse ofício é de grande antiguidade. Menciona-se o “sabão do pisoeiro” (Malaquias 3:2) e “o campo do pisoeiro” (2 Reis 18:17). Na transfiguração de nosso Senhor, diz-se que suas vestes eram brancas “como nenhum pisoeiro na terra as poderia branquear” (Marcos 9:3). En-rogel (q.v.), que significa literalmente “fonte do pé”, tem sido interpretado como a “fonte do pisoeiro”, porque ali os pisoeiros pisavam o tecido com os pés.

Texto original (inglês)

The word "full" is from the Anglo-Saxon fullian, meaning "to whiten." To full is to press or scour cloth in a mill. This art is one of great antiquity. Mention is made of "fuller's soap" (Mal. 3:2), and of "the fuller's field" (2 Kings 18:17). At his transfiguration our Lord's rainment is said to have been white "so as no fuller on earth could white them" (Mark 9:3). En-rogel (q.v.), meaning literally "foot-fountain," has been interpreted as the "fuller's fountain," because there the fullers trod the cloth with their feet.
