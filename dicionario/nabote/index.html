Nabote

Easton · em inglês: Naboth

Nabote, “o jezreelita”, era dono de uma porção de terra na encosta oriental do monte de Jezreel (2 Reis 9:25, 26). Esse pequeno “pedaço de terra” parece ter sido tudo o que ele possuía. Era uma vinha situada “junto ao palácio de Acabe” (1 Reis 21:1, 2), que o rei cobiçava muito. Nabote, porém, recusou-se, em qualquer condição, a vendê-la ao rei. Ele a havia herdado de seus pais, e nenhum israelita podia legalmente vender sua propriedade (Levítico 25:23). Jezabel, esposa de Acabe, ficou gravemente ofendida com a recusa de Nabote em desfazer-se de sua vinha. Por meio de um plano astuto e cruel, ela providenciou sua morte. Seus filhos também compartilharam o seu destino (2 Reis 9:26; 1 Reis 21:19). Em seguida, ela foi até Acabe e disse: “Levanta-te, toma posse da vinha, pois Nabote não está vivo, mas morto”. Acabe levantou-se e foi ao jardim que tão traiçoeira e cruelmente fora adquirido, aparentemente desfrutando da nova posse, quando, eis que Elias apareceu de repente diante dele e pronunciou contra ele um temível juízo (1 Reis 21:17-24). Jeú e Bidcar estavam com Acabe naquela ocasião, e as palavras de Elias ficaram tão profundamente gravadas na memória de Jeú que, muitos anos depois, ele as recorda (2 Reis 9:26). Foi ele o principal instrumento na execução dessa sentença contra Acabe, Jezabel e toda a sua casa (9:30-37). A casa de Acabe foi extinta por ele. Jeú não poupou nenhum de seus grandes homens, nem seus parentes, nem seus sacerdotes (10:11).

Texto original (inglês)

Fruits, "the Jezreelite," was the owner of a portion of ground on the eastern slope of the hill of Jezreel (2 Kings 9:25, 26). This small "plat of ground" seems to have been all he possessed. It was a vineyard, and lay "hard by the palace of Ahab" (1 Kings 21:1, 2), who greatly coveted it. Naboth, however, refused on any terms to part with it to the king. He had inherited it from his fathers, and no Israelite could lawfully sell his property (Lev. 25:23). Jezebel, Ahab's wife, was grievously offended at Naboth's refusal to part with his vineyard. By a crafty and cruel plot she compassed his death. His sons also shared his fate (2 Kings 9:26; 1 Kings 21:19). She then came to Ahab and said, "Arise, take possession of the vineyard; for Naboth is not alive, but dead." Ahab arose and went forth into the garden which had so treacherously and cruelly been acquired, seemingly enjoying his new possession, when, lo, Elijah suddenly appeared before him and pronounced against him a fearful doom (1 Kings 21:17-24). Jehu and Bidcar were with Ahab at this time, and so deeply were the words of Elijah imprinted on Jehu's memory that many years afterwards he refers to them (2 Kings 9:26), and he was the chief instrument in inflicting this sentence on Ahab and Jezebel and all their house (9:30-37). The house of Ahab was extinguished by him. Not one of all his great men and his kinsfolk and his priests did Jehu spare (10:11).
