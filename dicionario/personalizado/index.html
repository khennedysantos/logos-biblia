Easton · em inglês: Custom

Um tributo imposto pelos romanos. Os cobradores de impostos eram chamados publicanos (q.v.), que tinham seus postos às portas das cidades e nas vias públicas, e no lugar destinado a esse fim, chamado “recebedoria de impostos” (Mateus 9:9; Marcos 2:14), onde cobravam o dinheiro que devia ser pago sobre certas mercadorias (Mateus 17:25). Esses publicanos eram tentados a exigir do povo mais do que era lícito e, em consequência de suas extorsões, eram objeto de grande ódio. Os fariseus não queriam nenhum trato com eles (Mateus 5:46, 47; 9:10, 11).

Um tributo ou imposto (q.v.) de meio siclo era pago anualmente por todo judeu adulto para o templo. Tinha de ser pago em moeda judaica (Mateus 22:17-19; Marcos 12:14, 15). Cambistas (q.v.) eram necessários para que os judeus que subiam a Jerusalém nas festas pudessem trocar sua moeda estrangeira por dinheiro judaico; mas, como a lei proibia exercer tal comércio para obter lucro (Deuteronômio 23:19, 20), nosso Senhor os expulsou do templo (Mateus 21:12; Marcos 11:15).

Texto original (inglês)

A tax imposed by the Romans. The tax-gatherers were termed publicans (q.v.), who had their stations at the gates of cities, and in the public highways, and at the place set apart for that purpose, called the "receipt of custom" (Matt. 9: 9; Mark 2:14), where they collected the money that was to be paid on certain goods (Matt. 17:25). These publicans were tempted to exact more from the people than was lawful, and were, in consequence of their extortions, objects of great hatred. The Pharisees would have no intercourse with them (Matt. 5:46, 47; 9:10, 11).

A tax or tribute (q.v.) of half a shekel was annually paid by every adult Jew for the temple. It had to be paid in Jewish coin (Matt. 22:17-19; Mark 12:14, 15). Money-changers (q.v.) were necessary, to enable the Jews who came up to Jerusalem at the feasts to exchange their foreign coin for Jewish money; but as it was forbidden by the law to carry on such a traffic for emolument (Deut. 23:19, 20), our Lord drove them from the temple (Matt. 21:12: Mark 11:15).
